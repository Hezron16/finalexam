Inclusive Preparedness

Disasters affect everyone, but not everyone has the same needs. A good plan names the people who may need extra help and decides in advance how they will get it.

Plan with people, not for them

Ask each person what they need and what has worked before. Make sure information about warnings and evacuation is available in more than one way: sound, light, text, pictures and a trusted person who can explain it.

Persons with disabilities

  • Visual: keep a talking or large-print contact list and a whistle; label supplies in braille or by touch.
  • Hearing: use a flashlight or vibrating alert; write key messages on cards; keep spare hearing-aid batteries.
  • Mobility: choose a ground-floor safe spot, plan who will help, and keep spare parts for wheelchairs and crutches.
  • Cognitive or psychosocial: use simple pictures and a calm routine; keep a familiar item in the go-bag.

Older adults

  • Keep a 7-day supply of medicines and a copy of prescriptions.
  • Name a neighbour who will check on them.
  • Include eyeglasses, dentures and a cane in the go-bag.

Children

  • Teach children their full name, address and a parent’s phone number.
  • Practise drills as a game, not a scare.
  • Pack comfort items and a favourite snack.
  • Republic Act 10821 requires children’s needs to be considered in emergency response.

Pregnant and nursing mothers

  • Know the nearest health centre or hospital.
  • Pack prenatal records, vitamins and baby supplies.
  • Ask the evacuation centre about private areas for nursing.

Indigenous and remote communities

  • Share warnings in local languages, by radio, by community leaders and by traditional signals.
  • Respect local knowledge about weather signs, safe ground and routes.
  • Include elders when you map hazards.

Low-income households

  • Prepare gradually, using what you already have.
  • Ask your barangay about free training, kits and hazard information.
  • Pool supplies with neighbours.

HandaPH

Handa means “ready” in Filipino. HandaPH helps Filipino families, schools and communities prepare for typhoons, floods, earthquakes, fires and volcanic eruptions. It supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.
