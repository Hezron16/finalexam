Volcano Guide

The Philippines has 24 active volcanoes. Communities near them need to know the alert levels, the danger zones and their evacuation route.

Know the risk

Volcanoes such as Mayon, Taal, Kanlaon, Bulusan and Pinatubo have erupted in living memory. Dangers include ashfall, lahar (fast-moving mud and debris), pyroclastic density currents and volcanic gases. Lahar can happen long after an eruption, whenever heavy rain washes loose volcanic material downstream.

Who to follow: PHIVOLCS, the Office of Civil Defense and your provincial DRRMO.

What to do

Before

  • Know the alert level and danger zones of your nearby volcano.
  • Prepare N95 masks, goggles and extra water for your go-bag.
  • Learn your evacuation route and the location of your evacuation centre.
  • Agree on a family meeting point outside the danger zone.

During

  • Follow evacuation orders from local officials immediately.
  • Stay out of the Permanent Danger Zone.
  • Stay away from river channels when it rains because of lahar.
  • Protect your lungs and eyes from ash.

After

  • Wait for PHIVOLCS to lower the alert level before returning.
  • Clean ash carefully and avoid breathing it.
  • Watch for lahar warnings during heavy rain.
  • Check on neighbours and share official information.

PHIVOLCS volcano alert levels

Volcano Alert Levels used in the Philippines
LevelMeaning
0Normal
1Abnormal: low-level unrest
2Moderate unrest
3Relatively high unrest; magmatic eruption possible
4Hazardous eruption imminent
5Hazardous eruption in progress

Many volcanoes also have a Permanent Danger Zone that people should not enter or live in at any alert level. Check PHIVOLCS for the zone around your volcano.

Ashfall safety

  • Stay indoors, close windows and doors, and cover vents.
  • If you must go out, wear an N95 mask (or a damp cloth over your nose and mouth) and goggles instead of contact lenses.
  • Cover water containers and food. Do not drink ash-contaminated water.
  • Clean ash off roofs carefully, because wet ash is very heavy. Wear a mask and avoid sweeping ash into drains.

Watch and learn

Make Your Family Emergency Plan

How to build a plan the whole household understands. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for HandaPH (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Talk together: include children and older family members.
  2. Pick meeting points: one near home and one outside your neighbourhood.
  3. List contacts: write family, barangay and emergency numbers on paper as a backup.
  4. Learn the routes: walk the way to your evacuation centre and note any barriers.
  5. Practice twice a year: rehearse and update the plan when your household changes.

In an emergency, call 911. For the latest advice, always follow your barangay, local DRRMO and official government advisories.

HandaPH

Handa means “ready” in Filipino. HandaPH helps Filipino families, schools and communities prepare for typhoons, floods, earthquakes, fires and volcanic eruptions. It supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.
