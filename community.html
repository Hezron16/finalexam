Barangay and Community

The barangay is the first line of response. When neighbours know their risks and help each other, whole communities recover faster.

How the system works

Republic Act 10121, the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010, creates disaster councils from the national level down to every barangay. Each barangay has a Barangay Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Committee, and cities and municipalities have a local DRRM Office (DRRMO).

The law organises work into four areas: prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery and rehabilitation.

What you can do

  1. Find your hazards. Use HazardHunterPH (external link) to check hazards at your address.
  2. Visit your barangay hall. Ask about the evacuation centre, early warning system and training.
  3. Map your street. Mark flood-prone spots, safe ground and households needing help.
  4. Volunteer. Join the barangay emergency response team, the Red Cross or a youth group.
  5. Practise together. Hold a drill with neighbours at least twice a year.

Early warning

Agree how warnings will reach everyone: sirens, loudspeakers, text, radio and door-to-door checks.

Neighbour check-ins

Pair each household that needs help with a neighbour who will check on them.

Training

Ask your DRRMO, the Philippine Red Cross or the Bureau of Fire Protection about first aid, CPR and fire safety classes.

HandaPH

Handa means “ready” in Filipino. HandaPH helps Filipino families, schools and communities prepare for typhoons, floods, earthquakes, fires and volcanic eruptions. It supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.
