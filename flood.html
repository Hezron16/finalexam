Flood Guide

Floods are the Philippines’ most frequent hazard. They can rise quickly, so know your risk and your route to higher ground before the rain starts.

Know the risk

Floods can come from long rain, a river overflowing, a dam release, a high tide or storm surge. Low-lying areas, places near rivers and estuaries, and communities with blocked drains are most at risk. Check your barangay hazard map or community page to see how to find your flood risk.

Who to follow: PAGASA flood advisories and rainfall warnings, and your local DRRMO.

What to do

Before

  • Find out if your home or street is flood-prone and where the nearest higher ground is.
  • Keep drains and canals clear of garbage.
  • Store documents and medicines in waterproof bags on a high shelf.
  • Prepare your go-bag and a plan to leave early.

During

  • Move to higher ground early; do not wait for the water to enter your home.
  • Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater.
  • If water enters your home, switch off the main power if it is safe to do so.
  • Listen to local officials and PAGASA for updates.

After

  • Return only when officials say it is safe.
  • Do not touch electrical items that were wet.
  • Clean and disinfect everything the floodwater touched.
  • Throw away food and drinking water that touched floodwater.

Common flood myths

“It is only ankle-deep, so it is safe.”

As little as 15 centimetres (about 6 inches) of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet. Floodwater also hides open drains, debris and live wires.

“Floodwater is just dirty water.”

It can carry germs, including the bacteria that cause leptospirosis. If you waded through floodwater, wash with soap and clean water and ask a health worker whether you need preventive treatment.

Watch and learn

Flood Safety: Turn Around, Stay Safe

How to stay out of danger before and during a flood. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for HandaPH (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Know your risk: find out if your street floods and where higher ground is.
  2. Move valuables up: lift documents, electronics and medicines early.
  3. Stay out of floodwater: never walk or drive through it.
  4. Go to higher ground: move to your meeting point or evacuation centre before the water rises.
  5. Switch off power: if water enters your home, switch off the main power if it is safe to do so.

In an emergency, call 911. For the latest advice, always follow your barangay, local DRRMO and official government advisories.
