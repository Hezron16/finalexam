Evacuation Guide

Evacuating early is safer than waiting. Knowing where to go, what to bring and what to expect makes leaving home much less stressful.

When to leave

  • When local officials or the barangay order an evacuation.
  • When you are in a flood-prone, landslide-prone, coastal or volcano danger zone and a warning is raised.
  • When your home is unsafe after an earthquake or fire.
  • When you feel unsafe and cannot get help: call 911.

Before you leave

  1. Grab your go-bag, medicines, phone and power bank.
  2. Switch off the main power and close the LPG valve if it is safe to do so.
  3. Lock your home and tell a neighbour or family member where you are going.
  4. Help children, older adults and persons with disabilities first; bring mobility aids.
  5. Take the route officials recommend and avoid shortcuts through water, slopes or bridges.

What to expect at an evacuation centre

Registration

Staff will record your household so family and relief workers can find you.

Shared space

Centres are often schools, gyms and barangay halls. Space can be tight. Ask about areas for families, women and children, and persons with disabilities.

Relief and health

Water, food and health services are provided where available. Tell health workers about medicines and conditions.

Safety

Keep valuables close, keep children within sight and report any harassment or unsafe situation to the camp manager or barangay officials.

Rules for pets vary by centre. Ask your barangay in advance where animals can be sheltered.

In an emergency, call 911. For the latest advice, always follow your barangay, local DRRMO and official government advisories.

HandaPH

Handa means “ready” in Filipino. HandaPH helps Filipino families, schools and communities prepare for typhoons, floods, earthquakes, fires and volcanic eruptions. It supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.
