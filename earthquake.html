Earthquake Guide

Earthquakes strike without warning, so the best protection is a habit: Drop, Cover and Hold On, then move to open ground when the shaking stops.

Know the risk

The Philippines lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is crossed by many active faults, including the Valley Fault System that runs through Metro Manila. PHIVOLCS reports earthquakes and measures their effects on the PHIVOLCS Earthquake Intensity Scale.

Who to follow: PHIVOLCS, your local DRRMO and the Office of Civil Defense. Nationwide Simultaneous Earthquake Drills are held every quarter.

What to do

Before

  • Practise Drop, Cover and Hold On with your family and at school or work.
  • Secure heavy furniture and shelves.
  • Know the safe spots and the exits in every room.
  • Keep a go-bag and flashlight where you can reach them.

During

  • Drop to your hands and knees.
  • Cover under a sturdy table, or protect your head and neck.
  • Hold on until the shaking stops.
  • Do not use elevators and do not run outside while it shakes.

After

  • Expect aftershocks; drop, cover and hold on again.
  • Check yourself and others for injuries; give first aid.
  • Check for gas leaks and damaged wiring; leave a damaged building.
  • Follow PHIVOLCS advisories.

Living near the coast? Think tsunami

If you feel a strong shake that lasts a long time, or you see the sea suddenly rise or pull back, move right away to higher ground or inland. Do not wait for an official warning, and stay there until PHIVOLCS says it is safe.

Watch and learn

Drop, Cover and Hold On

What to do in the first seconds of an earthquake. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for HandaPH (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Drop: get down on your hands and knees right away.
  2. Cover: get under a sturdy table or desk, or cover your head and neck with your arms.
  3. Hold on: hold the table leg and stay put until the shaking stops.
  4. Then move: walk calmly to an open area, away from buildings, posts and glass.
  5. Stay informed: expect aftershocks, check PHIVOLCS advisories and call 911 for emergencies.

In an emergency, call 911. For the latest advice, always follow your barangay, local DRRMO and official government advisories.
