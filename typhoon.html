Typhoon Guide

Typhoons bring strong wind, heavy rain, floods and storm surge. Because they are forecast days ahead, preparing early saves lives.

Know the risk

About 20 tropical cyclones enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility each year, so most Filipinos will face one. Strong wind can tear off roofs, heavy rain can cause floods and landslides, and storm surge threatens coastal communities.

Who to follow: PAGASA, your local DRRMO and your barangay announcements.

What to do

Before

  • Check your roof, windows and doors; trim loose branches.
  • Pack your go-bag and charge phones and power banks.
  • Store 3 days of drinking water and food.
  • Agree on a meeting point and evacuation route with your family.

During

  • Stay indoors, away from windows and glass.
  • Follow PAGASA bulletins on a battery or crank radio.
  • Evacuate right away when local officials tell you to.
  • Stay away from the coast and from flooded rivers.

After

  • Wait for the official all-clear before going outside.
  • Stay away from fallen power lines and damaged buildings.
  • Boil or treat drinking water if the supply may be contaminated.
  • Check on neighbours, especially older adults and persons with disabilities.

PAGASA Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals

PAGASA uses five wind signals. A higher number means stronger winds and a greater threat to life and property. Always check the latest bulletin, because signals can be raised or lowered quickly.

Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS) and what they mean
SignalWind speedLead timeThreat to life and property
No. 139–61 km/h36 hoursMinimal to minor
No. 262–88 km/h24 hoursMinor to moderate
No. 389–117 km/h18 hoursModerate to significant
No. 4118–184 km/h12 hoursSignificant to severe
No. 5185 km/h or more12 hoursExtreme

Rainfall warnings

  • Yellow: flooding is possible. Monitor the weather.
  • Orange: flooding is threatening. Be ready to evacuate.
  • Red: serious flooding is expected. Take action now.

Check the latest bulletin on the PAGASA website (external link).

Storm surge: the hidden danger

A storm surge is a rise of sea water pushed ashore by a typhoon. It can reach far inland and can be deadlier than the wind. If you live along the coast and officials order a preventive evacuation, go early to a safe place on higher ground and farther from the shore.

Getting Ready Before a Typhoon

Five things to do when a typhoon is approaching. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for HandaPH (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Watch the bulletins: follow PAGASA and your barangay announcements.
  2. Secure your home: close windows and doors and move loose items indoors.
  3. Check your go-bag: water, food, medicines, flashlight, radio, documents and a charged power bank.
  4. Know where to go: know the way to your evacuation centre and leave early when officials say so.
  5. Stay connected: charge phones, write down emergency numbers and share your plan with family.

In an emergency, call 911. For the latest advice, always follow your barangay, local DRRMO and official government advisories.
